# Evidence And Scope

This page tells you what the repo demonstrates, which claims have outside support, and where the public evidence stops.

The working hypothesis is that AI-native data leadership depends on trusted metrics, operating cadence, review habits, and decision-ready communication. The operating artifacts, playbooks, examples, and public sources let you inspect that hypothesis from different angles.

## Operating Thesis

The repo has a consistent point of view:

- AI adoption is an operating-model problem
- trust, validation, and governance belong in the core design
- data leadership should connect workflows to decisions

## Boundaries

The repo does not publish private enterprise operating documents, a company's exact architecture or KPI system, or confidential business outcomes. It also cannot substitute for references, interviews, or deeper diligence.

Every pattern is generalized and rewritten for public use. A reader should not infer that one named company deployed each pattern exactly as written.

## Evidence Ladder

Use this ladder to calibrate each kind of artifact.

| Evidence layer | What exists here | What it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public point of view | README, essays, leadership writing | thesis and strategic clarity |
| Operating artifacts | operating system, board brief, scorecards, checklists | reusable management systems |
| Workflow examples | synthetic example, validation-oriented playbooks | practical patterns for AI-assisted work |
| Illustrative operating scenarios | synthetic before/after patterns with explicit labels | systems thinking and transferability, not deployment proof |
| External public credibility | fellow profile, third-party case study, company-authored technical scope, peer community pages | public reputation, role scope, and partial outcome validation |
